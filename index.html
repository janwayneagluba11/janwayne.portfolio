Chassis & Human Interface

As part of the Chassis & Human Interface sub-team, I designed and integrated the vehicle’s roll cage and driver-focused systems.

I used SolidWorks to develop and refine the chassis geometry while coordinating mounting locations and packaging requirements with the suspension, drivetrain, and other vehicle subsystems. I also use FEA to evaluate the chassis under front, side, rear, and rollover loading conditions and guide design iterations based on stress, deformation, and structural efficiency.

  • Designed and optimized roll cage and chassis, balancing structural strength, weight reduction, driver ergonomics, and manufacturability
  • Reduced chassis weight through optimization of tube geometry, member placement, and overall vehicle packaging while maintaining required structural performance
  • Redesigned engine mounting geometry and evaluated vibration behavior to improve power train integration and reduce vibration transmitted through the chassis.
  • Designed chassis components with fabrication in mind, accounting for tube cutting, notching, bending, fixturing, and welding
  • Participated in design reviews and cross-functional engineering discussions to justify design change.

Out of 77 cars, UCI placed 21st, just behind San Diego State at 20th and UCLA at 17th in the 2026 Baja SAE Competition in Oregon

My Work

One contribution I’ve made was CADing the chassis 2026-2027 season. Using our current Corsair design, I’ve worked with different subteams, such as suspension to fix the geometry of the front toe box smaller. This allowed us to have an overall smaller chassis profile.

I’ve also conducted all FEAs impact simulations and engine vibration analysis on the chassis using ANSYS.

Simple ideas

Design a chassis that is lighter and small but still meet all Baja SAE rules

Lasting impact

  • Smaller chassis - 36.6% weight reduction
  • Lower center of gravity
  • Better packaging

Fire-Extinguishing Rover

Led a team of engineers in creating a extinguishing rover. As a sub-team lead, I was leading the research, design, and prototyping and testing. Our design for this rover was a 1/10 scale RC crawler that would be able to spray water, hitting a target from multiple distances and target sizes.

This project strengthened my leadership skills and on-the sport problem solving skills. It also strengthened my communication skills.

My biggest role was ensuring all parts of the rover were correctly integrated and work around issues that occurred. Another role was ensuring our 4WD system was working properly meaning understanding how the transmission and transfer case worked together to power the axles. Additionally, making sure the turn servo was strong enough to turn the axles

Secondly, I designed a turret system that allows full rotation for the water pump system. I was able to achieve a full rotation, have multiple mounting points onto the chassis, and create minimal water revisor onto the chassis

Precision Drone

Designed and improved a precision drone aimed at having high navigation and reliable performance. My role involved CAD modeling, prototyping and collaborating with teammates to integrate mechanical system with electronics and controls.

This project strengthened my skills in design iteration, teamwork, and apply engineering principles to real-world challenges.

  • CAD models of drone components with focus on weight reduction and structural stability
  • Prototyped and assembled to test components fit and overall system integration
  • Participated in design reviews and iterative testing to refine performance

My Work

One of my contributions was the craw mechanism of our drone. The main goal of our design was compatibility but strong enough to carry and hold a 2×2×2 block cube.

We 3D printed our claw mechanism with PETG as it was stronger than TPU and still light weight. Our design accounted for the blocks thickness. It also accommodated a smaller lightweight servo. The claw mechanism attached to the bottom of our drone frame with the servo inside the hardware casing.

Sony Camera CAD

Designed a fully detailed CAD model of the Sony A6000 mirrorless camera in SolidWorks by reverse engineering the product from reference images and videos. The project focused on developing advanced parametric modeling skills, assembly designed, and designed for manufacturing principles while accurately recreating the camera’s geometry and functional components. The completed assembly consists of multiple individually modeled parts with working mechanical features, demonstrating proficiency in complex CAD modeling, mechanical assemblies, and engineering documentation.

Biggest takeaways: More than “I modeled a camera”

  • Reversed Engineering: I recreated a real product from reference images/videos rather than starting from supplied engineering drawings
  • Advanced SolidWorks Modeling: I developed a complex geometry using parametric features and multiple modeling techniques
  • Assembly Design: Created multiple individual components and brought them together into a functional assembly, demonstrating understanding of mates, interfaces, clearances, and component relationships.
  • Functional Mechanical Design: Features such as a movable screen, dial, battery, detachable microphone, and interchangeable components show that I modeled functionality, not just exterior geometry
